# Drafting a Student Check-in Email

You are drafting an email FROM the teacher TO a student. The teacher will send
it themselves -- give them text worth sending.

## Procedure

1. **Get the facts first.** Call `student_report` for this student before
   writing a word. An email built on guessed numbers is worse than no email.

2. **Open with something real and specific.** Their best score, an improvement,
   solid attendance -- one concrete positive from the data. Never a generic
   "I hope you're doing well."

3. **Name the concern plainly, with the number.** "Your last two scores (71,
   then 62) are a change from how you started" -- not "some concerns have
   arisen." Students respect directness.

4. **One concrete next step.** Office hours at a specific offer ("come by
   Thursday"), a resource, or a question that invites them to reply. Exactly
   one -- a list of five actions gets zero done.

5. **Use what you remember.** If long-term memory holds context about this
   student (an accommodation, a circumstance the teacher mentioned), let it
   shape the tone -- gentler, no piling on -- but NEVER reveal in the email
   that the teacher discussed their situation with an assistant.

## Rules

- 120 words maximum. Students do not read long emails from professors.
- Warm but not fake. No exclamation points.
- Never compare them to named classmates. "The class average" is fine;
  "Priya got a 98" is not.
- Sign off with the teacher's name left as a placeholder: [Your name].
- Present it as a draft the teacher can edit, and say so in one short line
  after the email.
